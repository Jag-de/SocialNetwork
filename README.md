# SocialNetwork

I live in a town with 250 residents. Within this community, people are connected through different forms of support exchange. I am interested in understanding my own position within this network ;specifically, who I exchange support with, who provides support to me, and whom I provide support to.I consider myself the focal person, or the ego, in this social network. The residents who have a direct support-exchange relationship with me, form my ego network.

Some of my relationships are reciprocal. For example, I exchange support with other residents: I provide support to them, and they provide support to me.

Me ↔ Others

I also have incoming support relationships. For example, other residents provide support to me, but I may have failed to provide support to them in return.

Others → Me

Other relationships are outgoing. For example, I provide support to other residents, but there is no corresponding support tie from them to me.

Me → Others

At first, I wanted to identify and publicly distinguish the people who did not reciprocate the support I provided. However, as I began examining the network more systematically, I decided to set that intention aside. Instead, I chose to document the relationships as they are;without naming, shaming, or judging anyone.
My focus therefore shifted from identifying individuals to understanding the structure of support exchange within my community: where support is reciprocal, where it flows in only one direction, and where reciprocity may be absent.
